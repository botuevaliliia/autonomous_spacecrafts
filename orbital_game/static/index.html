YOUR FLIGHT GUIDE

Meet Aster. Stay together.

Aster starts at a new position on each attempt. Lyra is waiting aboard: reach her station and match its velocity, like catching a train moving alongside you.

Win when both are true at the same momentDistance ≤20 km and relative speed ≤30 m/sThe flight stops and “Rendezvous complete” appears automatically. Overlapping icons alone do not mean a win.
  1. Plan a push. Choose burn strength and direction. A “burn” instantly changes your velocity; m/s is the size of that change.
  2. Read the forecast. The mint path and “After proposed burn” box show what would happen if you execute that burn now. They are not your current measurements.
  3. Execute, then coast. Execute burn spends your maneuver budget. Play lets gravity carry you; pause anytime. After a burn, Coast to closest approach pauses at the predicted nearest encounter within the next three hours.
  4. Match Aster’s motion. Check Distance now and Relative speed now. Another burn is usually needed to reduce the velocity difference. Being close but moving fast is a flyby.

You have 800 m/s of maneuver budget and 5 simulated hours. Avoid Mars. The time multiplier only changes how quickly you watch the flight. Opening this guide pauses playback.

How to practice with a new starting position

Timing is part of the puzzle. Aster stays on the same outer orbit but starts somewhere new. The same immediate burn will not meet it every time. Coast before departure, pause, and compare the predicted closest distance for your planned burn. Watch how that prediction changes as the two ships move. Execute when you like the encounter, then match the station’s motion near arrival.

Every starting position is chosen to allow a two-burn rendezvous within the five-hour limit and the original budget. New attempt gives a new position; pausing or opening this guide does not.

What do the direction buttons mean?

Prograde (0°) points along the local orbital tangent in the starting direction of travel; Retrograde (180°) points the other way. Outward (+90°) points away from Mars; Inward (−90°) points toward Mars. On an elliptical orbit the tangent direction is not necessarily your exact velocity direction. Use the slider or click the map for angles in between.

01 / GRAVITY

Always falling. Always missing.

Mars pulls inward while your sideways motion carries you forward. The pull gets weaker with distance: double the distance from Mars’ center and gravity is one quarter as strong.

02 / KEPLER

Orbits have a rhythm.

Bound, unpowered paths are ellipses, including circles. A ship moves faster near Mars and slower farther away. Larger orbits take longer to complete.

03 / RENDEZVOUS

One burn is only the start.

A prograde push from the lower circular orbit raises the opposite side of your path. You slow as you climb. Near the higher orbit, another prograde push can match the station’s velocity.

Real gravity, with deliberate simplifications

The server integrates a = −μr / |r|³ for both objects, with steps no larger than one simulated second. The engine in use appears in the game footer: Basilisk, or the Newtonian numerical fallback.

Mars is a stationary spherical body. Burns are instantaneous. We omit atmosphere, moons, irregular gravity, finite engine firing, and relativity. This is a two-body orbital model and a generous rendezvous target, not a docking simulation.

Ship icons are enlarged; distances on the map share one scale. Tests check orbital periods, energy and angular momentum during coasting, and the analytic two-burn transfer.
